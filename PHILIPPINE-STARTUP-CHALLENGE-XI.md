## I. SUMMARY

VOXSTOCK is a proposed technology startup that addresses a critical information gap during flooding in the Philippines: weather apps can tell you *that* it is raining, but they cannot tell you whether *your specific road* is still passable. Our flagship product, **Passable Ba?**, combines low-cost IoT water-level sensors deployed on flood-prone roads with a mobile application that translates real-time water measurements into simple, understandable road statuses — **Passable, Caution, High Water, or Not Passable** — categorized by vehicle type.

Our mission is simple: before a motorcycle rider, commuter, parent, or delivery driver enters a flooded street, they should already know what waits for them there.

---

## II. BACKGROUND OF THE PROBLEM

The Philippines experiences approximately 20 tropical cyclones annually, and flooding is among the most frequent and destructive consequences, affecting millions of Filipinos every year.

> **Source note:** the ~20 cyclones per year figure is **PAGASA**'s — it is a meteorological measurement of the Philippine Area of Responsibility. **NDRRMC** publishes impact data (persons affected, damage, casualties), not cyclone frequency. Both are cited correctly below.

During heavy rain, existing tools leave road users dangerously uninformed:

- **Weather apps** give city- or province-level forecasts, not street-level road conditions.

- **Navigation apps (e.g., Waze, Google Maps)** rely on crowdsourced user reports, which are inconsistent, delayed, and unavailable in low-traffic or low-income areas — precisely where flooding information matters most.

- **Government flood advisories** are broadcast broadly but rarely indicate which specific roads are impassable and for which vehicles.

The result is a recurring, preventable scenario: riders and drivers unknowingly enter flooded roads, leading to stalled vehicles, damaged engines, missed work and school, stranded commuters, and in severe cases, loss of life. The people most affected — daily wage earners, delivery riders, students, and commuters without private vehicles — are also those with the least capacity to recover from these disruptions.

The core problem is not the absence of data. It is the absence of **localized, real-time, easy-to-understand road condition information.**

> **What already exists (we acknowledge this openly):** PAGASA and DOST operate automated rain gauges and water-level sensors, and river-level monitoring is real and valuable — Marikina City, for example, runs a three-stage alarm system at the Sto. Niño Bridge gauge (15 m prepare · 16 m evacuate · 18 m forced evacuation). Academic work on local flood-warning systems also exists (e.g. ALeRTO in Zamboanga Peninsula). Passable Ba? does not replace any of it. We propose to add the layer those systems do not cover: **street-level passability, in plain language, for the person about to enter the water.**

## III. PROPOSED STARTUP SOLUTION

**Passable Ba?** is a proposed flood-intelligence platform composed of two parts:

**1. Hardware — VOXSTOCK Water-Level Sensors**

Low-cost, weather-sealed IoT sensors mounted on poles or road fixtures beside flood-prone roads. Each sensor continuously measures water depth and transmits readings to the cloud.

**2. Software — Passable Ba? Mobile App & Dashboard**

A mobile application (and web dashboard for organizations) that displays nearby monitored roads on a map, color-coded by status:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 🟢 Passable | Safe for all vehicles |
| 🟡 Caution | Passable with care |
| 🟠 High Water | Risky; small vehicles avoid |
| 🔴 Not Passable | Do not enter |

Each status is evaluated by VOXSTOCK's **Passability Engine**, which converts raw water-level measurements into per-vehicle recommendations (motorcycle, car, truck), updated in near real-time.

## VIII. MARKET ANALYSIS

**Market Context:**

- The Philippines is one of the most flood-prone countries in the world, with flooding affecting millions annually, making flood information a *yearly, nationwide* demand — not a niche.

- The country has a massive daily road-user base: millions of motorcycle riders (including a rapidly growing delivery-rider workforce), commuters, and logistics vehicles — all with smartphones.

- The Philippines also has among the highest social media and mobile internet usage rates globally, supporting rapid app adoption.

**Demand Drivers:**

- Increasing frequency and intensity of rainfall events linked to climate change.

- Growth of the delivery and logistics economy, whose workers are paid per trip and cannot afford flooded-route delays or vehicle damage.

- LGU mandates for disaster preparedness and climate adaptation spending.

**Competition:** Existing solutions are indirect (weather apps, crowdsourced navigation). River-level monitoring by PAGASA, DOST and LGUs is real but focused on waterways rather than individual roads. No dominant localized, sensor-based **street-level passability** platform currently serves ordinary Filipino road users — representing a first-mover opportunity at the community level.

> The team will conduct primary market validation during the MVP phase. No market-size figure is claimed here because none has been independently sourced yet.

- Build redundancy: local data storage, backup connectivity, battery/solar power options.
