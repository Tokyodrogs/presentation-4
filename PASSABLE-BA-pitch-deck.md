## CORRECTIONS APPLIED TO THE BRIEF

Two changes were made to protect credibility with the judges. Both are deliberate.

1. **Removed the "drowning statistics" slide.** The original brief's Slide 9 was a statistics slide. Unverifiable drowning figures were dropped entirely and replaced with **Challenges & Solutions** — the version below. The deck now cites only two sources, both verifiable, both on-screen.
2. **Screens 4, 5 and 8 carry honesty labels.** Existing PAGASA/DOST river gauges are acknowledged on Slide 4 rather than ignored; the app interface on Slide 5 is labelled a mockup; the pilot cities on Slide 8 are labelled examples only. A judge who knows the flood-monitoring landscape will look for exactly these, and finding them scored higher than not finding them.

# SLIDE 1 — YOU'RE 2 KM AWAY

**1) Scene (what we see)**
POV from behind a motorcycle delivery rider working a flooded Manila street at night. *Foreground:* rain streaks on the lens, gloved hands on handlebars, a spray of water kicked up by the front wheel. *Midground:* the road vanishing under rising water — lane markings disappearing one by one. *Background:* soft silhouettes of an overpass and a row of houses, sodium streetlight halos bloomed into haze. A food-delivery box is strapped behind the rider.

**2) Composition & depth cues**
Low over-the-shoulder angle. 35mm anamorphic feel, slight barrel vignette. One-point perspective straight down the road, vanishing point just right of centre. Three clean depth planes: rain bokeh (blurred, foreground) → rider (razor sharp, midground) → city (soft, background). Headline sits in the upper-left negative space, never over the rider.

**3) On-screen text (exact)**
- **Build-in sequence** (one line at a time, 1.2s apart, each fading as the next arrives):
  - `YOU'RE 2 KM AWAY.`
  - `IT'S RAINING.`
  - `YOUR MAP SAYS THE ROAD IS OPEN.`
  - `BUT IS IT?`
- **Then** (hold 2 seconds, everything else fades out):
  - **Headline:** `VOXSTOCK`
  - **Product:** `PASSABLE BA?`
  - **Tagline:** `Know the road before you take it.`
- **Body:** none. This slide is wordless apart from the sequence.
- **Footer:** TRIVOX · Grade 12 – James Gosling · SABNAHIS — plus SABNAHIS seal, bottom-right, 14% opacity.

**4) Color & material notes**
Near-black blue everything. Cyan appears in exactly one place — a thin light-spill along the rider's shoulder to seed the palette. Amber from the sodium streetlamps. **Zero red on this slide.** Wet asphalt carries a long vertical reflection of the headlight.

**5) Motion/transition cue**
Slow dolly-in through the rain; the water on the lens wipes across frame right and becomes the split of Slide 2.

**6) Do/Don't guardrail**
Don't reveal the logo until the last beat — the room must feel the problem before they meet the brand. No clip-art rain, no motion blur on the text.

# SLIDE 2 — THE INFORMATION GAP

**1) Scene (what we see)**
A hard 50/50 vertical split. *Left:* a hand holding a phone in the rain, the screen the only light source, showing a generic weather app reading `HEAVY RAIN`. *Right:* the same street, now visibly flooded — brown water crossing the road past a tricycle's wheels. *Bridging the seam:* three frosted glass cards floating at three different depths, each carrying one unknown.

**2) Composition & depth cues**
Perfect vertical division, seam dead-centre. The glass cards straddle the seam so the eye is forced to travel across the gap — that crossing *is* the argument. Nearest card is sharp and bright; the furthest is blurred and dim. Text anchors top-left.

**3) On-screen text (exact)**
- **Headline:** `THE WEATHER KNOWS IT'S RAINING.` (5 words)
- **Sub-headline:** `BUT DOES IT KNOW YOUR ROAD?` (6 words)
- **Body:** `General rain warnings tell you the storm is coming. Your street, your underpass, your route — still unknown.` (18 words)
- **UI labels / callouts (glass cards, exact):**
  - `WATER LEVEL: UNKNOWN`
  - `ROAD CONDITION: UNKNOWN`
  - `MOTORCYCLE: ?` / `CAR: ?` / `ALTERNATE ROUTE: ?`
- **Citation chip** (small, lower-right, always visible): `SOURCE: PAGASA — an average of 20 tropical cyclones enter the Philippine Area of Responsibility each year; about 8–9 cross the country.`
- **Footer:** TRIVOX · Grade 12 – James Gosling · SABNAHIS

**4) Color & material notes**
Left half is cold — cyan glow from the phone screen lighting the fingertips. Right half is warm and dirty — amber streetlight on brown floodwater. The three glass cards are the only bright objects: frost + 12px blur, 1px cyan edge light. Amber is used strictly for the "unknown" state. No red.

**5) Motion/transition cue**
Camera pushes *through* the centre seam, the glass cards passing either side of lens, resolving into the sensor on Slide 3.

**6) Do/Don't guardrail**
Never say weather apps don't work — they do, for rain. This slide is an *information gap*, not a competitor attack. In the speaker notes, name it that way: "general weather information vs street-level passability."

# SLIDE 3 — THE REVEAL

**1) Scene (what we see)**
Hero product shot. A realistic IoT water-level sensor — roughly the size of an electrical junction box, matte grey plastic, brushed metal bracket, IP-rated cable gland, antenna stub — mounted on the curb wall of a flood-prone underpass. Water sits at 40 cm, still and reflective. Beside the sensor, a cyan holographic readout hangs in the air: `WATER LEVEL: 40 CM`. A thin cyan light stream peels off the sensor and travels right, toward the word CLOUD.

**2) Composition & depth cues**
85mm lens, shallow depth of field. Sensor occupies the right third, tack sharp. Left two-thirds is deliberate negative space for the hologram and the headline. One-point perspective runs along the wet curb edge, pulling the eye toward the vanishing point behind the sensor. Hologram floats at a *different* depth to the sensor so the slide never reads as flat.

**3) On-screen text (exact)**
- **Headline:** `PASSABLE BA?` (2 words)
- **Body:** `Street-level flood information, when you need it.` (8 words)
- **UI labels / callouts (exact data chain):**
  - `SENSOR` → `📡` → `VOXSTOCK CLOUD` → `PASSABLE BA?`
  - Hologram readout: `WATER LEVEL: 40 CM`
- **Footer:** TRIVOX · Grade 12 – James Gosling · SABNAHIS

**4) Color & material notes**
The palette turns fully on here. Cyan owns the slide — hologram, data stream, sensor edge light. Wet asphalt mirrors the hologram downward so the reflection doubles the glow. The sensor itself stays desaturated and utilitarian: this must look *installable and affordable*, not sci-fi. No amber, no red.

**5) Motion/transition cue**
Camera tracks along the cyan data stream leaving the sensor, and the stream straightens into the vertical spine of Slide 4's exploded diagram.

**6) Do/Don't guardrail**
Do not render the sensor as alien hardware. It should look like something a barangay could realistically mount on a wall — a box, a bracket, a cable. Judges must believe it can be deployed, not admire it.

# SLIDE 4 — SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE (NO JARGON)

An exploded-view 3D diagram on a dark field. Five glass slabs stacked along a vertical axis, each one floating slightly apart from the next, each labeled on its right edge. **2) Composition & depth cues**
Isometric exploded stack, each layer on its own one-point plane so the stack has real depth rather than reading as a list. Labels right-aligned to a shared vertical axis for instant readability. The bottom slab (USER) is largest and closest to the viewer — the diagram literally comes toward the audience at the end. A faint vertical light beam runs behind the stack.

**3) On-screen text (exact)**

- **Body:** `A sensor measures. A network sends. A server organises. A rule decides. A person acts.` (16 words)

  2. `CONNECTIVITY` — `Sends the number`

- **Honesty chip** (small, beneath the stack, always visible): `ALREADY EXISTS: PAGASA and DOST river gauges monitor river level — e.g. Marikina City's three-stage alarm at the Sto. Niño Bridge gauge (15 m prepare · 16 m evacuate · 18 m forced evacuation). PASSABLE BA? proposes what they don't cover: street-level passability. Source: Rappler; Manila Bulletin.`

# SLIDE 9 — CHALLENGES & SOLUTIONS (THE DARKEST SLIDE)

**1) Scene (what we see)**
The loneliest frame in the deck. A single sensor mounted on a concrete post at night, in heavy rain, lit by one amber work-light from above. Rain hammers the housing. Water is rising past the bracket. Around the sensor, eight dark warning cards float at staggered depths, each carrying one risk in amber type and its answer in cyan type. The city is invisible — this frame is about the thing being exposed.

**2) Composition & depth cues**
Sensor small and off-centre (left third) — deliberately under-sized, because the risks are the subject. Cards arranged in a loose orbit at four depths, nearest sharp, furthest soft. Card hierarchy is strict: risk line in amber, then a thin divider, then the solution in cyan. Two-column text alignment inside each card for fast scanning.

**3) On-screen text (exact)**
- **Headline:** `WE DON'T IGNORE THE RISKS.` (5 words)
- **Sub-headline:** `WE DESIGN AROUND THEM.` (4 words)
- **Body:** `Every failure below has an answer built into the plan.` (10 words)
- **UI labels / callouts (exact — all eight cards):**

  | Risk (amber) | Answer (cyan) |
  |---|---|
  | `Sensor theft` | `Secure mounting + barangay partnership` |
  | `Vandalism` | `Protected enclosure` |
  | `Bad readings` | `Calibration + multiple measurements` |
  | `Network failure` | `Local storage + backup connectivity` |
  | `Power failure` | `Battery / solar backup` |
  | `Permission` | `Partner with barangay / LGU / property owner` |
  | `Maintenance` | `Scheduled inspections` |
  | `False alerts` | `Data validation + sensor health monitoring` |

- **Footer:** TRIVOX · Grade 12 – James Gosling · SABNAHIS

**4) Color & material notes**
The amber work-light is the only warm source in the entire deck, and it is doing narrative work: this is the failure scenario. Soaked concrete, beaded water on the housing, a cold ambient fill from an unseen cyan source suggesting the system is still alive. Card edges: 1px amber for risk, 1px cyan for answer. Absolutely no decorative colour — legibility outranks atmosphere on this slide.

**5) Motion/transition cue**
The rain intensifies and instantly stops; the water on the concrete drains away, revealing the clean map surface of Slide 10.

**6) Do/Don't guardrail**
Do not stylise the cards into unreadability. Every risk-answer pair must be legible from the back row. This slide exists to prove the team has thought realistically about deployment — if a judge can't read it, the slide fails at its only job.

# SLIDE 10 — FINAL VISION

**1) Scene (what we see)**
A return to the exact road of Slide 1 — same street, same night, same rain — except the rider has stopped short of the flood. On the handlebar, the phone shows `🔴 NOT RECOMMENDED`. Ahead, an alternate route lights up as a cyan line curving away from the water. Then the camera lifts: the road surface dissolves upward into a digital city map, and across it, hundreds of sensor points ignite one by one like stars coming out. Fade to black.

**2) Composition & depth cues**
Opens on the identical framing as Slide 1 so the audience feels the loop close. The camera's vertical pull upward is the only large movement in the entire deck — earned, and saved for this moment. Sensors ignite outward from the centre in a slow radial ripple, cyan, each with a soft bloom. Type sits in the dark, left-aligned, and holds for two full beats before the fade.

**3) On-screen text (exact)**
- **Build-in sequence:**
  - `DON'T JUST KNOW IF IT'S RAINING.`
  - `KNOW IF THE ROAD IS PASSABLE.`
- **Headline:** `PASSABLE BA?` (2 words)
- **Body:** `Real-time road conditions, turned into information people can act on.` (11 words)
- **Tagline:** `Know before you go.`
- **Lockup (small type, bottom):** `VOXSTOCK` · `TRIVOX — Versoza, Azhley Nicole D. · Abique, Breian I. · Profeta, Dref Nicolo F.` · `Sablayan National Comprehensive High School (SABNAHIS)` · `Grade 12 – James Gosling`
- **Footer:** TRIVOX · Grade 12 – James Gosling · SABNAHIS

**4) Color & material notes**
Everything reduces to navy and cyan. The single red element in frame is the phone's `NOT RECOMMENDED` warning — one red dot on a screen, small, and it is the reason the rider stopped. Amber is gone. As the map rises, cyan points multiply until the screen is at its brightest, then the fade to black takes everything with it.

**5) Motion/transition cue**
Fade to black. No outro slide, no credits roll, no applause frame. Silence after the fade is part of the pitch.

**6) Do/Don't guardrail**
Never add a "Thank You" slide after this one — it would destroy the ending. The final frame is black.
